1. Proposal and located source

Virtual environment for space travel and intergalactic species evolution. The located historical proposal is a 14-page PDF whose cover states 30 September 2026 as its preparation date. It proposed two planets and a space zone. The original remains unchanged in the original project. This is a Theory edition with corrected identification and an updated description; it does not establish that the proposal was submitted to Classroom before 20 October.

The product lets users control Rek’Sai and a ship across surfaces and space. It combines rock and crystals, forest and an ocean wave with a base, track, capsules, satellite and hangar. Fictional-universe references describe the academic prototype’s visual inspiration; they do not imply an official franchise production.

Current scope checked against implementation

Four experiences remain: planet, racing, flight and observation. Worlds are separate states, entered through proximity/interaction and left through portals or zone transitions. The avatar is animated through parts. There are procedural rockets, a keyframed rocket, doors, panels and computed trajectories. TARS is confirmed in Main.cpp and Tars.cpp. Gohan, domes and other planned models are not treated as completed merely because they appear in the proposal.

Theory completion criteria

Completion requires seven justified animations, a functional camera, coherent lighting and transforms, reviewed geometry/textures, an executable Release and bilingual individual documentation. Models + Textures must remain below 100 MB. The GitHub link must support rebuilding the project. Videos, a video blog, ZIP archives and PDB files are not added as requirements of this Theory rubric.

Conclusion

Updating the proposal explains changes without rewriting the past. The project moved from two planned surfaces to three zones with distinct experiences. Its academic value lies in connecting models, matrices, animation, lighting and interaction within one scene. Submission claims must be supported by concrete tests while retaining portability and visual-review limitations.
